---
title: LEFT JOIN
description: Preserve every row on the left and match records on the right when they exist — and avoid the WHERE clause that quietly turns it back into an INNER JOIN.
---

## Syntax

```sql
SELECT ...
FROM left_table lt
LEFT JOIN right_table rt
  ON lt.key = rt.key;
```

(`LEFT OUTER JOIN` is the same thing — `OUTER` is optional.)

## What it does

Keeps every row from the left table, matched or not. Right-side columns are
`NULL` where there's no match.

<SqlRunner query={`SELECT c.customer_id, c.name, o.order_id, o.amount
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
ORDER BY c.customer_id;`} />

Customers 6 and 7 show up with `NULL` order columns — unlike
[INNER JOIN](/docs/joins/inner-join), they aren't dropped.

## The classic bug: a WHERE filter collapses it back to INNER

<SqlRunner query={`SELECT c.customer_id, c.name, o.order_id, o.status
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
WHERE o.status = 'paid';`} />

Customers 6 and 7 vanished. `o.status = 'paid'` runs in `WHERE` **after** the
join, and `NULL = 'paid'` is `UNKNOWN` — so every unmatched row gets filtered
out, and the `LEFT JOIN` behaves exactly like an `INNER JOIN`. See
[WHERE: LEFT JOIN filters belong in ON](/docs/core/where#left-join-filters-belong-in-on)
for why.

Move the condition into `ON` to keep the unmatched rows:

<SqlRunner query={`SELECT c.customer_id, c.name, o.order_id, o.status
FROM customers c
LEFT JOIN orders o
  ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
  AND o.status = 'paid'
ORDER BY c.customer_id;`} />

Same filter, same join — but now it's part of *which rows match*, not *which
rows survive*, so customers 6 and 7 stay with `NULL` order columns.

## Safety check before shipping

- confirm whether unmatched rows are expected in the output
- decide whether right-side `NULL`s should be coalesced or left explicit
- make sure any condition on the right table is in `ON`, not `WHERE`, unless collapsing to an inner join is the actual intent
